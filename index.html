Unit tests for LLM answers. Assert the claims, cite the source.

Paste a source of truth, write what the model said as assert "claim" lines, and each claim comes back PASS (the source says so), FAIL (the source says otherwise) or SKIP (the source doesn't say).

01how it works

Every claim gets three pieces of evidence and a three-way verdict.

  1. split

    Sentences

    The source is cut on . ! ? followed by a space and a capital letter, so “Python 3.0” and “$50.00” stay whole.
  2. context

    Premises

    Each sentence is glued to the sentences its pronouns point back to, plus the document's first sentence, so “It was first released in 1991” knows what “it” is.
  3. retrieve

    Top-3 by cosine

    all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeds premises and claims; the three closest premises become the claim's evidence.
  4. judge

    NLI cross-encoder

    nli-deberta-v3-xsmall (int8 ONNX) scores each (premise, claim) pair. Any entailment ≥ 0.5 → PASS, else any contradiction ≥ 0.5 → FAIL, else SKIP.

Both models run through transformers.js 3.8.1 on onnxruntime-web (WebAssembly, one thread, since GitHub Pages can't send the cross-origin-isolation headers needed for more). The NLI label order is read from the model's own config.json (0 contradiction, 1 entailment, 2 neutral) rather than hard-coded. There's no server: the page is static files and the weights come from the Hugging Face hub.

Timings are from headless Chrome 155 on my Windows desktop (16 logical cores, but the WASM backend uses one). The first click in the UI also pays a one-time warm-up, so it took 2.2–3.7 s. Accuracy was identical on every repeat. You can re-run the whole eval in your own browser at eval.html.

03what I learned

The model was fine. The sentences I was feeding it weren't.

  • Pronouns break sentence-level NLI, and retrieval too. With bare sentences the sample scored 4/8: in my first version “It was created by Guido van Rossum” didn't even make the top 3 for “Python was created by Guido van Rossum”, because the embedding doesn't know what “it” is.
  • “Previous sentence” isn't enough. Adding just the previous sentence (6/8) fails on chains like “It was created… It was first released in 1991”. Walking back to the last sentence that names its subject, plus the document's first sentence for “The language…”, got 7/8.
  • Context has a price. Gluing the first sentence everywhere means “The tower is 330 metres tall” can “prove” the tower is in Paris. The verdict is right, but the evidence card is less precise, so I show the context in grey instead of hiding it.
  • Off-topic contradictions are common. “Python 3.0 was released in 2008” contradicts “Python was first released in 1991” with 0.95. I let any strong entailment win before looking at contradictions: with previous-sentence premises that scored 6/8 vs 5/8 for “take the larger of the two”; with the final premises both rules score 7/8.
04limits

A small model with a bias towards “false”.

  • Silent ≠ false, but it often says false. 2 of 4 SKIP claims came back FAIL. Treat a FAIL as “go check”, not as proof.
  • 22 labelled claims is a small eval. It shows the failure modes; it can't give a precise accuracy.
  • No real numeric reasoning. Mismatched years worked here (“completed in 1899” vs “1887 to 1889”), but ranges, units and arithmetic are not checked.
  • One claim per line, judged alone. Long, compound claims (“A and B”) should be split; the splitter also breaks on abbreviations like “Dr. Smith”.
  • The LLM answers are hand-written. I wrote the claims to look like typical model output; they aren't sampled from a real model.
